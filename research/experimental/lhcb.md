The Large Hadron Collider at CERN is the world's largest particle accelerator.
**LHCb** is one of its four main experiments. It specialises in particles that
contain *b* ("beauty") and *c* ("charm") quarks. How these particles are
produced and decay is very sensitive to new particles too heavy to make
directly. This lets LHCb probe energy scales far above the LHC's collision energy.

## What we do at Monash

The Monash group was founded as a theory group. It began experimental work in
2017, and LHCb is now one of its two main experiments. Through the
**Monash Warwick Alliance in Particle Physics**, we work closely with the
University of Warwick's LHCb group.

Our current activities include:

- **Rare decays and lepton universality.** We test whether electrons, muons
  and taus really behave identically in the decays of *b* hadrons, including
  in electroweak penguin decays.
- **Real-time data selection.** LHCb reads out the full detector at the LHC
  collision rate and selects events in software. We apply machine learning
  to make those decisions faster and better.
- **Computing and simulation.** The group is responsible for how LHCb uses
  event generators, and for the software that gives collaborators worldwide
  access to the experiment's distributed data.

The Monash theory group works on the same physics from the other side,
building the models and simulations the experiment tests.
