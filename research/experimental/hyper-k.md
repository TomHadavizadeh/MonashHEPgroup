Neutrinos are the most abundant matter particles in the Universe, and the
least understood. They change from one type to another as they travel, a
process called *neutrino oscillation*. If neutrinos and antineutrinos
oscillate differently, this *CP violation* could help explain why the
Universe is made of matter rather than antimatter.

**Hyper-Kamiokande** (Hyper-K) is the third generation of the Kamiokande
water Cherenkov detectors, following the Nobel Prize-winning Kamiokande and
Super-Kamiokande. Its far detector is being built about 600 m underground in
Hida City, Japan: a cylindrical tank 68 m across and 71 m deep, holding
258,000 tonnes of ultra-pure water. Its fiducial volume is about eight times
that of Super-Kamiokande. Tens of thousands of photomultiplier tubes line the
tank, recording the faint rings of Cherenkov light produced when a neutrino
interacts in the water. The experiment is expected to begin taking data in 2028.

## Physics goals

- **Neutrino CP violation.** An upgraded, 1.3 MW neutrino beam from the J-PARC
  accelerator in Tokai is sent 295 km across Japan to the detector, to compare
  how neutrinos and antineutrinos oscillate.
- **Proton decay.** Grand unified theories predict that protons are not
  completely stable. Hyper-K will search for their decay with unprecedented
  sensitivity.
- **The neutrino mass ordering**, using neutrinos produced in the atmosphere.
- **Neutrino astronomy**: neutrinos from the Sun, and from supernova explosions
  in our galaxy and across cosmic history.

## What we do at Monash

Monash researchers work on reconstructing what happened in each interaction
from the recorded light, including machine-learning methods that make event
reconstruction faster and more accurate.
